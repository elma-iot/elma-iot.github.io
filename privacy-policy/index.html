About this policy

This Privacy Policy describes the current ELMA IoT Android application, labeled ELMA Flasher, developed by Elnur Mehdiyev under ELMA IoT. Its Android application ID is com.elma.iot.flasher. It also explains privacy when visiting this website or contacting support.

Contact the developer at elik745i@gmail.com for privacy questions or requests.

Current Android version: 1.0.0. The app stores configuration on your phone and communicates with destinations described below. Live advertising and new in-app purchases are disabled in the current build. Google Play purchase checks and online radio-directory requests still exist.

Data stored on your phone

The app uses private Android app storage and WebView browser storage for saved configurations and interface preferences. Depending on what you use or enter, these can include:

  • Device names, board and peripheral settings, Wi-Fi network names and passwords, MQTT broker details and credentials, and device web-access settings.
  • The last device address you opened and paired-PC information: its URL, certificate fingerprint and access token.
  • Designer layout, selected tabs and profiles, radio selections, recent playback entries, and file-browser preferences or caches when those interfaces are used.
  • A verified Google Play purchase record and signature, when available, to restore an ad-free entitlement.

Wi-Fi scan results, active USB connections, firmware bundles and operation logs are also processed during the relevant session. The app does not automatically upload your saved configuration to a developer cloud account.

Private app storage is access-restricted by Android. The app does not add its own encryption to saved preferences. Its manifest disables Android backup, and its extraction rules exclude app data from cloud backup and device transfer.

Permissions &amp; device access

Wi-Fi and location

On request, the app uses Android’s precise and approximate Location permissions to scan nearby Wi-Fi networks. Android also requires Location services to be enabled. The app reads network names (SSIDs), signal strength, frequency and security capabilities to show compatible networks; it can also display the connected Wi-Fi name and local IP address. It does not request GPS coordinates or background location tracking. Raw scan lists are not uploaded by the app. A network name you choose can become part of the configuration you later export or send to your PC or ESP device.

USB and ongoing operations

Android asks you to grant access to a selected USB device. The app reads USB device, vendor, product and port information, and communicates with the ESP bootloader to identify the chip, check flash capacity, write firmware and apply configuration. Foreground-service permissions support ongoing data-transfer and connected-device operations.

Files and media

Import and export use Android’s system document picker. The app accesses files you select, such as configuration documents and firmware bundles; a connected device’s web interface can also offer file uploads or media controls. The app does not request broad storage, photo-library, camera or microphone permissions and does not record audio or video. Exported configurations and firmware bundles may contain credentials.

Other permissions

Internet and network-state permissions support the connections in this policy. Included SDKs add billing, advertising-ID, Ad Services and wake-lock permissions, as well as an internal receiver-protection permission. The current app has no Bluetooth or Nearby Devices permission or phone-side Bluetooth pairing feature. Bluetooth and microphone choices in the hardware Designer describe ESP peripherals, not access to your phone’s Bluetooth radio or microphone.

Where information is sent

Your paired PC

When you choose Compile, the app sends your saved configuration, including any credentials it contains, to the PC endpoint you paired. It sends a pairing token for authentication and receives progress, logs and a firmware bundle. This connection uses HTTPS with the paired certificate fingerprint checked. The PC may retain configuration and build files; manage those copies separately on that PC.

Your ESP device

USB provisioning sends configuration and firmware to the selected ESP. Device mode opens the HTTP or HTTPS address you supply, initially suggesting 192.168.4.1. OTA sends a firmware image to the address you enter and can send device-login credentials using HTTP Basic authentication. It checks device status and upload progress. The app does not provide an automatic LAN-wide ESP discovery service; Wi-Fi network scanning and USB enumeration are separate features. MQTT discovery publishing belongs to the connected ESP interface.

Your MQTT broker

The Android MQTT connection test sends authentication details you enter to your selected broker, with a randomly generated client ID for that connection. It checks connectivity; the phone-side test does not publish device discovery or runtime commands. The broker receives the network connection and handles it under its own practices.

Radio Browser

The packaged Designer requests the country list from all.api.radio-browser.info when it starts, if the network is available. A selected or previously saved country is sent when loading its station list. These requests expose your IP address and normal HTTP request information to the directory service. A selected country is a browsing preference, not a GPS reading. See Radio Browser.

Connected-device pages and external content

Device mode displays the web interface served by the address you choose. That page can make its own network requests. The shared interface includes an optional artwork lookup that sends track title, artist, album or filename search terms to Apple’s iTunes Search API and retrieves an artwork image. This is a media-preview feature; the packaged Android Designer hides device-storage views and does not run artwork searches at startup. When using a device page that offers it, the receiving service sees the search and connection information. See Apple’s privacy information.

External links, including GitHub project and support links, open the destination’s services. Their providers receive ordinary connection information. The app does not control the content or data practices of arbitrary device pages, brokers or external websites you choose.

ELMA IoT servers: the current app contains no developer-operated cloud account, configuration-upload, analytics or crash-reporting endpoint. The paired compiler is the PC you select, not a centrally configured ELMA IoT server. This does not mean that no information leaves your phone: Google services, Radio Browser and your chosen destinations receive the information described here.

Google services, ads &amp; purchases

The app includes Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) 25.4.0, Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) 4.0.0 and Google Play Billing 9.1.0.

Advertising is currently disabled. The app’s advertising flow does not initialize Mobile Ads or load banner ads while its live-monetization flag is off. Public Google test ad identifiers are configured. The SDK and its manifest components remain included, and app-measurement initialization is delayed. Inclusion of these components is not evidence that the current app is serving ads.

Google documents that, when its Mobile Ads SDK is in use, it can collect and share IP-derived approximate location, device or account identifiers (including advertising and app-set IDs), app/ad interactions and diagnostics for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Those are Google’s SDK disclosures, not a claim that an ad request occurs in this disabled build. See Google’s Mobile Ads data disclosure and Google’s Privacy Policy.

UMP is included for consent and advertising privacy choices. The normal consent-update flow is also gated by the disabled flag. The menu at Files → Remove ads / purchases → Advertising privacy choices can invoke Google’s privacy-options interface; a form may not be available in this build.

Purchase checks remain active. The app connects to Google Play Billing and queries existing purchases on startup/resume and when you choose Restore purchases. A valid existing purchase can be acknowledged using its purchase token. The app checks the product, package and signed purchase record locally, and can retain a verified receipt and signature. New checkout for the one-time elma_remove_ads product is blocked in this build.

Google Play manages its account and transaction records. ELMA does not implement its own account registration or sign-in and does not receive payment-card details. Device or broker usernames are for those destinations, not an ELMA account.

No separate Firebase Analytics, Firebase Crashlytics, Sentry or similar developer analytics/crash-reporting integration was found in the current app. Google SDKs and services have their own processing and diagnostics; the app’s disabled advertising flow should not be read as a promise that third-party services exchange no data.

Other included libraries

Eclipse Paho MQTT 1.2.5 makes the broker connection described above. Its app logger discards log messages. USB Serial for Android 3.10.0 and the packaged esptool-js code communicate with your selected USB device. fflate and @noble/hashes handle firmware archive and checksum processing locally. These library integrations do not add a developer telemetry endpoint.

The shared browser flasher source also references UNPKG and jsDelivr for browser-only dependencies. The native Android Build screen uses packaged libraries, and the packaged pages restrict scripts to their own origin. Those CDN references are not an automatic download by the native Android flashing flow.

Connection security

Paired-PC compilation uses certificate-pinned HTTPS. MQTT uses TLS on port 8883 or when the TLS option is supplied; ordinary MQTT uses unencrypted TCP. Device mode and OTA support both HTTP and HTTPS. On HTTP connections, device credentials, configuration and other transferred information may be readable on the network. USB communication is a direct connection to the selected hardware.

Not all app communications are encrypted in transit. Use trusted devices and networks, and choose HTTPS or TLS where the destination supports it. Keep pairing tokens, configuration exports and firmware bundles private.

Retention, deletion &amp; your choices

Saved app data persists until you replace it, clear the app’s storage in Android Settings, or uninstall the app. Some session state and logs last only for the current session; a verified purchase may be restored again from Google Play.

  • Revoke Location permission in Android Settings if you do not want Wi-Fi scanning. The app explains the permission before requesting it.
  • Clear app storage to remove saved configurations, paired-PC details, receipts and WebView data held by the app.
  • Delete exported files separately from the document provider or folder you selected. Clearing the app does not delete copies on an ESP, a paired PC, a broker or an external storage service.
  • Manage Google account, purchase and provider records through Google. Clearing local app data does not cancel a purchase or erase Google’s records. Advertising ID controls are available in Android Settings.
  • Contact the provider of a broker, device page or other external service about information that it holds. Their retention periods are not controlled by the Android app.

For information you have sent to the developer by email, contact elik745i@gmail.com to request access, correction or deletion. Support correspondence is used to respond to your request and retained as needed to resolve it or meet applicable obligations. There is no ELMA cloud account to delete.

This website &amp; contacting support

This static website has no ELMA analytics scripts, advertisements, sign-in, tracking pixels or contact form. Its fonts and visual assets are served with the site. It is hosted on GitHub Pages: GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security, as described in its Pages documentation and Privacy Statement.

When you email support, the developer receives your email address and whatever message or attachments you choose to send. Your email provider and the recipient’s email provider process that correspondence. GitHub issues are public; do not post credentials, private configurations, pairing tokens or purchase receipts there.
